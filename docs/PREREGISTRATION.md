# Pre-registration

This file is committed and tagged `prereg-v1` before any revision experiment is run on GPU. The OOD pair sets, the job registry (`python -m ddiood.gpu_jobs --list`) and the analysis below are frozen at that tag. Any later change is recorded in a dated amendment at the end of this file.

## Claim

SemEval 2013 DDI classifiers succeed by reading annotated interaction sentences, not by encoding knowledge about the drugs. Their scores therefore carry no information about interactions of drugs that are not described by a sentence, and a random-split evaluation cannot reveal this.

## Frozen evaluation sets

| Set | Definition | Size |
|---|---|---|
| SemEval test | official test split, pairs whose two entities are `drug` or `drug_n` | 2,810 pairs |
| Novel OOD | single-ingredient drugs first approved 2020 to 2024, absent from SemEval and the training vocabulary; positives from the drug's own FDA Drug Interactions section; partner-matched negatives (each negative reuses a positive's partner with a different novel drug whose label never names it) | 438 pairs, 148 positive, 48 drugs |
| Older OOD | same construction for drugs first approved 1985 to 2012 that are absent from SemEval but present in the PubMed pretraining era | 1,944 pairs, 648 positive, 135 drugs |
| Random-negative variants | the same positives with negatives drawn uniformly from the vocabulary, kept to show why partner matching is needed (a partner-popularity prior reaches AUROC 0.86 on them) | `*_random_negatives.csv` |
| Name swap | every SemEval test pair with both drugs replaced by other vocabulary drugs, by novel drugs, or by pseudo-words, sentence otherwise unchanged | 3 x 2,810 |
| OOD swap | Novel OOD positives that have an FDA co-occurrence sentence, each paired with a copy whose partner is replaced by a true negative partner of the same novel drug | 100 |
| Drug-disjoint | 30% of SemEval test drugs held out from training (seed 0); evaluation on the 1,271 test pairs that contain a held-out drug | 1,271 |

Hashes of the OOD files are in `results/corrected/ood_summary.json` and `results/corrected/ood_old_summary.json`.

## Reference baselines (computed before any model run)

`results/corrected/baselines.json`, AUROC on Novel OOD: all-positive 0.500; partner-frequency prior 0.508; novel-drug prior 0.643; CYP3A keyword rule 0.539; partner named anywhere outside the Drug Interactions section of the novel drug's label 0.797; DrugBank lookup 0.949 (circular for label tier B, reported on tier A only). Every model is reported next to these rows.

## Primary endpoint

AUROC of P(interaction) for the BiomedBERT model trained with mixed inputs (`b2mixed`), evaluated on Novel OOD with names-only input (empty sentence), averaged over seeds 1, 2, 3, 4 and 42, with a 95% bootstrap interval that resamples novel drugs.

## Hypotheses and decision rules

Equivalence margin for "no signal": AUROC in [0.40, 0.60], tested with two one-sided tests at alpha 0.05 using the drug-cluster bootstrap interval. Holm correction across H1 to H6.

| ID | Hypothesis | Supported if | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | No transferable drug knowledge | primary endpoint interval inside [0.40, 0.60] | interval lower bound above 0.60 |
| H2 | Predictions are driven by the sentence | on SemEval test, masked-entity binary F1 is at least 90% of unmasked F1, and fewer than 10% of binary predictions change under each name swap | masked F1 below 75% of unmasked, or more than 25% of predictions change under a name swap |
| H3 | The model does not use which drug the sentence is about | `b2sentence` AUROC on OOD swap inside [0.40, 0.60] | interval lower bound above 0.60 |
| H4 | The failure is not explained by absence from pretraining | primary model AUROC on Older OOD names-only inside [0.40, 0.60] | Older OOD lower bound above 0.60 while Novel OOD is inside the margin; the claim is then narrowed to pretraining coverage |
| H5 | The OOD set can reward a model that has the answer (positive control) | KnowledgeRAG-DDI with the Drug Interactions section indexed reaches OOD AUROC at least 0.75 and MCC at least 0.30 | below either threshold; all null results are then reported as uninterpretable |
| H6 | Retrieval over the four allowed label sections does not recover signal | KnowledgeRAG-DDI (400 characters per passage, top 5) OOD AUROC inside [0.40, 0.60] | interval lower bound above 0.60, reported as a positive finding |

## Secondary analyses

Drug-disjoint SemEval results; architecture comparison (BiomedBERT, bert-base-uncased, BioBERT); entity-masked training; BioMistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct label-word probabilities with and without retrieved passages; B4 logistic regression; leakage controls in `results/corrected/controls.json`; DrugBank agreement (label tier B) and the human audit (label tier C) once annotated; per-drug cap sensitivity; confusion matrices for every system; AUPRC against prevalence, MCC and balanced accuracy for every cell.

## Reporting commitments

Every number in the revision is produced by `python -m ddiood.evaluate` from saved prediction files that record the job config hash and the OOD pair hash. Results that contradict the claim are reported with the same prominence as results that support it.

## Amendments

Amendment 1 (2026-10-16, before any retrieval-based job ran). The retrieval caches shipped with `prereg-v1` were built for the earlier random-negative pairs, so the partner-matched negatives had no retrieved passages while every positive did. This would have let any retrieval system separate the classes by the presence of passages alone. Retrieval, the B4 logistic regression, the leakage controls and the GPU bundle were rebuilt for the frozen 438-pair set. Jobs that use no retrieval (E1, E2, E3, E6 and the names-only LLM jobs) are unaffected. The OOD pair sets themselves are unchanged.

Amendment 2 (2026-10-16). Implementation details that the plan left open, recorded as run: the primary endpoint averages P(interaction) across seeds per pair and computes one AUROC on the averaged scores (not the mean of per-seed AUROCs); encoder training uses at most 5 epochs with early stopping on validation macro F1, except the retrieval probe, which uses at most 10; mixed-input training uses two copies of each pair (with the sentence, and with an empty sentence).

Amendment 3 (2026-10-16). DrugBank agreement and the DrugBank lookup baseline were recomputed on the frozen set: 133 of 148 positives are confirmed, and DrugBank lists an interaction for 106 of 289 resolvable partner-matched negatives. Label tier B (FDA and DrugBank agree) is therefore reported for every system, not only as a secondary analysis.

Amendment 4 (2026-10-16, filed before predictions from seeds 1, 2, 3 and 4 were analysed; only seed 42 had been examined). An adversarial review and a power analysis (`scripts/power_analysis.py`, `results/corrected/power_analysis.json`) showed three design limits, so the analysis is amended as follows.
1. Statistic. Every OOD hypothesis (H1, H4, H6) uses the within-drug AUROC: positives and negatives are compared only within the same novel or older drug, which removes the drug-level prior that partner matching leaves (novel-drug prior AUROC 0.64) and the below-chance artifact it can produce. Intervals remain two-way cluster bootstrap intervals over novel drugs and partners. Pooled AUROC is reported alongside.
2. Older cohort. Seven entries in the Older set were leaks or parse artifacts: six salt forms of SemEval vocabulary drugs (almotriptan malate, atazanavir sulfate, atorvastatin calcium, citalopram hydrobromide, darifenacin hydrobromide, rosuvastatin calcium) and the string "benzoate". Their rows are removed from all Older analyses ("Older-clean"); the remaining pairs are unchanged.
3. Power and decision rules. With 48 novel drugs the 90% interval half-width of a null scorer is about 0.15, so the Novel cohort can never place its interval inside the 0.40 to 0.60 margin, and label noise (DrugBank lists an interaction for 37% of resolvable negatives) attenuates a true AUROC of 0.70 to about 0.60. The Novel cohort is therefore reported as an estimate with a one-sided 95% upper bound, not as an equivalence test. The equivalence test of H1 (no transferable pair knowledge) is moved to Older-clean, where a null scorer falls inside the margin 95% of the time and a true AUROC of 0.60 is distinguishable. H4 becomes the comparison between cohorts (Novel estimate against Older-clean estimate). A pooled Novel plus Older-clean analysis is secondary.
4. H3 (OOD swap) is reported descriptively only: because negatives never carry a co-occurrence sentence, the swapped negatives still assert an interaction, so H3 cannot separate a sentence reader from a model with drug knowledge.
5. Label noise. DrugBank agreement is computed for the Older cohort as for the Novel cohort, every OOD result is reported on label tier A and tier B, and the attenuation implied by the DrugBank disagreement rate is reported with each estimate.

Amendment 5 (2026-10-16, filed before predictions from seeds 1, 2, 3 and 4 were analysed). Three corrections to the record, none of which changes a decision rule set out above.
1. The DrugBank lookup baseline under "Reference baselines" reads 0.949. That figure came from the earlier random-negative pairs. On the frozen partner-matched Novel set the DrugBank lookup scores AUROC 0.767 (95% interval 0.691 to 0.839); `results/corrected/baselines.json` and `artifacts/ood/drugbank_validation.csv` hold the corrected value.
2. Results are produced by `python -m ddiood.evaluate2`, the evaluator that implements Amendments 1 to 4, not by `ddiood.evaluate`.
3. "Holm correction across H1 to H6" is made concrete. Only hypotheses decided by an equivalence test carry a p-value, and H3 is descriptive under Amendment 4, so the Holm family is H1, H4 and H6. H2 and H5 are decided by their stated thresholds and are reported without adjustment.

Amendment 6 (2026-10-16, filed before predictions from seeds 1, 2, 3 and 4 were analysed). Operational definitions needed to implement Amendments 4 and 5 in `ddiood.evaluate2`.
1. H4 compares cohorts, so it is tested on the difference in within-drug AUROC, Older-clean minus Novel, with independent cluster bootstraps per cohort. H4 is supported if the difference is equivalent to zero within plus or minus 0.10 (half the width of the 0.40 to 0.60 margin) at alpha 0.05 and the Older-clean 95% lower bound is at most 0.60. H4 is falsified if the Older-clean 95% lower bound exceeds 0.60 while the Novel one-sided 95% upper bound is at most 0.60. Otherwise H4 is inconclusive.
2. H6 keeps its original rule and its equivalence p-value, and its Novel one-sided upper bound is reported beside it. At 48 Novel drugs a supported decision is not expected to be reachable, and this is stated with the result.
3. A falsified decision for an equivalence hypothesis requires the 95% lower bound above 0.60. Equivalence p-values use the 90% bootstrap tails.
4. Label-noise attenuation is reported from the negative-side DrugBank contradiction rate r as observed = (1 - r) x true + 0.5 r, at a true AUROC of 0.70.
5. H2b applies the H2 rule to the masked-trained model: its masked-test F1 relative to its own sentence-test F1.
6. In the within-drug bootstrap, a drug drawn more than once enters as separate strata, one per copy, so that each copy carries the same weight as an independently drawn drug. The earlier implementation merged copies into one stratum. The power analysis is re-run with the corrected bootstrap.

Amendment 7 (2026-10-16). Filed after all preregistered analyses were complete and after the decisions for H1, H4, H5 and H6 had been observed. Every analysis below is post hoc and exploratory and changes no preregistered decision: H5 remains falsified, and H1, H4 and H6 remain inconclusive. No outcome of E7, E8 or E9 changes the H5 decision. The analysis code, input builders, prompts, thresholds and the hashes listed in item 9 were fixed at the commit tagged `prereg-v1-amend7`, before any E7, E8 or E9 job ran; item 10 records an earlier draft of this amendment that was committed before that freeze. Seed 42 of the original models had been examined before Amendment 4 was filed, which is why Amendment 4 exists; the originally preregistered primary endpoint (pooled Novel OOD AUROC of the names-only `b2mixed` model, averaged over seeds 1, 2, 3, 4 and 42, drug-cluster bootstrap) is reported with equal prominence beside every result of this amendment. They test whether a pipeline can read an answer that is placed in its input, not whether any model knows drug interactions. The SemEval test pairs of E7b use the contaminated retrieval, because many test mentions do not map to a label.

3. E7, the encoder pipeline on pair labels.
Hypothesis (exploratory): BiomedBERT trained on binary pair labels derived from Drug Interactions sections reads an answer that is in its input. The H5 input is not shown to cap performance: an audit found that a partner-visibility rule on the H5 input scores 0.635 within-drug, while an ideal reader of that input, one that also rejects negatives whose visible partner mention is not an interaction statement, reaches 0.775 within-drug (0.785 pooled), above the H5 threshold of 0.75. The H5 failure is therefore a model failure on reachable input, and E7a asks whether pair labels remove it. Training mechanics are identical for every arm: BiomedBERT, binary head, class weights [1, 2] (not the 5-class weights of `ddiood/config.py:28`), learning rate 2e-5, batch 16, 10 epochs at most with patience 2, a validation split grouped by anchor drug (10% of anchors, both classes required), model selection on validation within-drug AUROC, seeds 42, 1, 2, 3 and 4. These mechanics differ from H5's (5-class head, class weights, random validation split, macro F1 selection); E7a therefore changes the labels and these mechanics together, which is stated with its result.
- E7a, like-for-like H5 repair: the H5 input unchanged (contaminated retrieval, 400 characters per passage, k = 5, `a [SEP] b [SEP] passages`), trained on the pair labels. It is judged against the H5 thresholds on the H5 cohort and statistic: Novel pooled AUROC of the five-seed average at least 0.75 and MCC of the majority vote at least 0.30.
- E7b, integrity control with fixed inputs: each passage is prefixed `source drug | section`, cut to a window of 320 characters centred on the first partner mention when there is one, and passages that name the partner are placed first; k = 5; own-label retrieval. Reachability R is the share of positives whose text, after the 512-token truncation, names the partner (partner synonyms by shared RxCUI). It was computed before any run on these exact inputs: R = 1.000 for Novel and 1.000 for Older-primary, and on the training pairs 0.999. The assertion R >= 0.95 holds. The pass threshold is T = max(0.75, 0.5 + 0.8 x (R - 0.5)): T = 0.900 for Novel and 0.900 for Older-primary. E7b passes if, on both Novel and Older-primary, the within-drug AUROC is at least T and its 95% lower bound is above 0.70. With the global contaminated retrieval of E7a the same fixed inputs reach only R = 0.655 (Novel) and 0.797 (Older-primary), and literal partner matching there also fires on partner-label passages for 23.1% of Novel negatives; that is why E7b uses own-label retrieval and E7a keeps the H5 retrieval.
- E7c names only, E7d other-labels retrieval, E7e clean retrieval (fixed input format as E7b): knowledge arms with no pass rule. Per arm and cohort the report gives the within-drug AUROC with its interval, kappa = (AUC - 0.5) / (AUC_DrugBank - 0.5) on the same rows, the training-label partner-prior scorer (smoothed positive rate of the partner in the training labels, `a7-prior-partner-train`), the label-free additive residual of `scripts/residual_analysis.py:49-56`, and paired differences between arms.
  - DrugBank lookup within-drug AUROC used for kappa, recomputed from the validation files: Novel 0.729, Older-clean 0.641, Older-primary 0.652.
  - "signal": the partner-residualized within-drug AUROC has a 95% lower bound above 0.5 AND the paired difference against the training-label partner-prior scorer has a lower bound above 0. "no signal": the within-drug AUROC is equivalent to [0.40, 0.60] by two one-sided tests at 0.05 on Older-primary. Otherwise "neither".
- Pair weighting and drug weighting are both reported. No bar below 0.75 is used anywhere in E7.

4. E8, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as an evidence reader.
- Frozen before any run (`ddiood/amend7_prompts.py`, hash `24f7ba2559aec7e09293092cc1d1a470ec6c3566858ba7f78ef72272321fdfdb`): the chat template of the tokenizer with one user turn and the generation prompt; one primary prompt (names or evidence, then "Answer with one word only: yes or no") scored as P(yes) normalized over the first tokens of yes and no (capitalized and spaced forms included, log-sum-exp within a class); 4 sensitivity prompts on names only (the five-way label prompt, a shuffled option order, two rewordings; label words false, enzyme, effect, warning, interaction, each a whole-word token, so no label depends on a word-piece prefix) scored as 1 - P(false) over the five label words. Prompts are never dropped. A pair is a non-answer if the label tokens carry less than 0.5 of the next-token mass; non-answers are counted and kept. The maximum prompt length is 8192 tokens; the run tokenizes without truncation and raises if any prompt is longer (the existing `ddiood/llm.py:99-100,125` truncates on the left). The model is loaded as in `llm.load_any`.
- Names only: the primary statistic is the within-drug AUROC of the primary prompt on Novel and Older-primary; the other four prompts are sensitivity only. "Robust" means every prompt's AUROC is within 0.05 of the primary. Per-prompt AUROC, minimum and maximum are reported.
- Evidence arms, all scored with the primary prompt on the evidence format of E7b (passages as `source drug | section: window`, k = 5 for the retrieval arms):
  - oracle: the drug's own Drug Interactions section, truncated to 6000 tokens. Integrity rule: within-drug AUROC at least 0.90 AND no more than 0.05 below the regex baseline (case-insensitive partner match with RxCUI synonyms in the exact context) on BOTH cohorts. The regex baseline is 1.000 on Novel and 1.000 on Older-primary, so the bar is max(0.90, regex - 0.05) = 0.950 on Novel and 0.950 on Older-primary. The primary statistic is the Older-primary 95% lower bound, which must reach its bar; on Novel (48 drugs, wide interval) the point estimate must reach its bar and the interval is reported.
  - name-masked oracle: the same section with every specific drug name replaced by `[DRUG]` (names of the vocabulary, of every OOD drug and of all labels in `data/fda_labels`, including brand names, plus every surface form of the pair's own partner) and the class statements kept; reported on tiers A and B.
  - wrong oracle: the Drug Interactions section of another OOD drug of the same cohort, one donor per drug, fixed seed 7. redacted oracle: the true section with every sentence that names the partner removed. The donor pool of the wrong oracle excludes the seven Amendment 4 names and the missed vocabulary drugs. Both are expected to fall to the names-only level of the primary prompt.
  - contaminated, other-labels and clean retrieval, top 5.
- Context statistics reported for every arm: prompt tokens (mean, maximum), evidence truncations, non-answers, and the share of positives (and negatives) whose context names the partner, from the run and from the pre-run audit (`input_audit.json`).
- Stem control (names only, primary prompt, partners real). A committed list of 96 stems (`ddiood/amend7_evidence.py`, hash `99455f208ef5a781da735ace08ae2cb12cf80734badb81df2e5f062a8693087a`) defines a stem-bearing drug. For each stem-bearing OOD drug three names are scored: the real name, a stem-preserving name (a seeded random prefix of the same length plus the real stem) and a stem-scrambled name (the same prefix plus a length-matched permutation of the stem letters that matches no listed stem, within one Qwen token of the preserved name). Names avoid every vocabulary and label name; `inputs.pseudo_word` is not used because its suffix is a kinase stem. Novel and Older-primary are pooled: 25 of 48 Novel and 55 of 121 Older-primary drugs have a stem (80 drugs; the others have no listed stem or a multi-word name and are not scored). Pre-stated expectation if the name-stem shortcut explains the names-only signal: the within-drug AUROC of the scrambled names is lower than that of the preserved names by at least 0.05, with a paired drug-cluster 95% interval that excludes 0; the preserved names stay near the real names.

5. E9, a panel trained with one recipe, replacing the search for public SemEval checkpoints.
- A Hub search on 2026-10-16 (queries and results in `artifacts/amend7/hub_candidates.json`) found three DDI-named candidates with unnamed labels (`jialicheng/ddi_biobert`, `jialicheng/ddi-pubmedbert-abstract`, `jialicheng/ddi-pubmedbert-fulltext`: labels LABEL_0 to LABEL_4, model card names no training corpus) and one multi-corpus model. Public checkpoints are therefore an external subset only, and only after: the label mapping is taken from the SemEval TRAIN majority class (the class predicted most often on 3000 training pairs is the negative class), a negative class exists, the card names no TAC 2018 or DrugBank training, and the input format is documented. Only wesin/pubmedbert-relation-extraction passes the static checks (computed from the model card, the label set and the README, not asserted); it is scored as a single external point and flagged if its SemEval score is above the published state of the art (the reference figure is recorded with the result).
- The panel: 20 models trained on SemEval with the recipe of `b2sentence` (`ddiood.bert.train`, 5 epochs at most, seed 42; weights from the pinned revision, PyTorch files only; cased vocabularies are loaded with `do_lower_case` false, recorded per repository in `panel_manifest.json`): ten backbones (BERT-base, BioBERT, BiomedBERT abstract, BiomedBERT abstract-fulltext, BioLinkBERT, SciBERT, Bio_ClinicalBERT, RoBERTa-base, DeBERTa-v3-base, ELECTRA-base; every repository id and revision hash is in `panel_manifest.json`, verified on 2026-10-16) in two input formats, a text pair (`sentence`, `a [SEP] b`) and an entity-marker sentence (`<e1>D</e1> ... <e2>P</e2>`, placed at the annotated entity offsets for SemEval sentences and at the first non-overlapping occurrences for FDA sentences; the separator inside the names text is the tokenizer's own separator token). Names-only input is the empty sentence with the names for the pair format and the neutral template `<e1>D</e1> and <e2>P</e2>.` for the marker format. 2 `@DRUG$`-masked models are left out of every correlation and reported separately (their names-only scores tie at exactly 0.5).
- Statistics: per model, SemEval test binary F1 and the names-only within-drug AUROC on Novel and Older-primary; the per-checkpoint rule is a 95% upper bound of at most 0.60 on Older-primary. The primary framing is the between-checkpoint variance of the OOD AUROC against the mean within-checkpoint bootstrap variance. Spearman's rho between SemEval F1 and OOD AUROC is descriptive only; its interval comes from the same two-way bootstrap over OOD drugs and partner drugs (seed 1042 for Novel and 42 for Older), with draws shared by all models; the between-checkpoint and within-checkpoint variances use those draws too.

This section lists changes to the analysis code made after results were seen, and implementation choices that the plan and Amendments 1 to 6 did not fix. None of them changes a preregistered decision. Commits are in this repository.

1. Evaluator changes after the five-seed results (commit f52959c, 2026-10-16 12:12, after the five-seed evaluation in commit 7f24d69 at 12:03).
   * The within-drug bootstrap for the Novel cohort now uses seed 1042; Older cohorts keep seed 42. Before the change both cohorts used seed 42, so the two cohort bootstraps drew from the same random stream; Amendment 6.1 asks for independent cluster bootstraps per cohort. Effect: H1-novel 95% interval [0.391, 0.647] became [0.397, 0.650] (90% [0.412, 0.628] became [0.411, 0.626]; estimate 0.523 unchanged); H4 interval [-0.212, 0.074] became [-0.220, 0.068] and its equivalence p-value 0.322 became 0.339 (Holm-adjusted 0.643 became 0.677 at the time; it is 0.339 now that H6 is complete). 2. Power analysis re-run (commit 7f24d69). Amendment 6.6 states that the power analysis is re-run with the corrected bootstrap. The re-run was committed together with the five-seed results, so the record does not show that it ran before those results were seen. It also raised the simulation size from 60 replicates with 200 bootstrap draws to 100 replicates with 300 draws. Figures quoted in Amendment 4 come from the earlier run. For a null scorer on Novel with clean labels, the 90% interval half-width changed from 0.148 to 0.111 and the mean one-sided 95% upper bound from 0.640 to 0.605. On the Older set it changed from 0.070 to 0.050, and the share of null runs whose 90% interval falls inside the 0.40 to 0.60 margin went from 0.95 to 1.00. With DrugBank noise applied to the Novel labels, a true AUROC of 0.70 is observed at 0.595 in the earlier run and 0.594 in the re-run. Neither run changes a decision rule.
3. Implementation choices not fixed by the plan or the amendments.
   * H5 MCC uses a majority vote across seeds: a pair is predicted positive when at least half of the five seeds predict an interaction class. H5 AUROC uses the seed-averaged probability (Amendment 2).
   * Binary predictions (used for F1, MCC, balanced accuracy and the H2 flip rates) are the argmax over the five SemEval classes, counted as positive when the argmax is not "false". They are not a threshold on P(interaction) = 1 - P(false).
   * In the two-way cluster bootstrap, partner resampling can leave a resampled drug copy with only positives or only negatives. Such a copy contributes nothing to that draw's within-drug AUROC and is in effect dropped. On average 19.6% of drawn drug copies are dropped per draw for Novel (median 18.8%, range 0% to 54%, seed 1042) and 12.0% for Older-clean (median 11.7%, range 1.6% to 28.9%, seed 42), over 1,000 draws. This is part of why the interval is conservative.
   * H2a and H2b aggregate seeds by the mean of the per-seed masked-to-unmasked F1 ratios and the mean of the per-seed flip rates; the rule is then applied to those means. The plan does not say how seeds are aggregated for H2. For H2b the seed mean ratio is 6.008 because seed 1 has a ratio of 23.94. The median ratio is 1.581, the ratio of mean F1s is 1.868 and the per-seed range is 1.364 to 23.939. Seeds 4 and 42 each exceed the 0.10 flip threshold for novel-drug and pseudo-word swaps (seed 4: 0.108 and 0.103; seed 42: 0.110 and 0.106). The seed-mean flip rates (0.064, 0.072, 0.072) are below it. `ddiood.evaluate2` reports these figures in the H2b detail and keeps the preregistered seed-mean value and decision.
4. Reporting additions in `ddiood.evaluate2`, made after the results were seen, that change no decision.
   * An Older-clean sensitivity row (H1-sensitivity, not preregistered and outside the Holm family). The Older-clean filter missed five SemEval-vocabulary drugs: clopidogrel bisulfate, perindopril erbumine, peginterferon alfa-2a, calcitonin and esmolol (78 rows). Without them, the within-drug AUROC is 0.453 (90% [0.394, 0.511], 95% [0.379, 0.522]), p_TOST 0.078, n = 1,754 pairs over 123 drugs. That is inconclusive, as H1 is.
   * In the markdown tables, the pooled-AUROC interval columns of the per-job and seed tables are renamed `auroc_pooled_lo95`, `auroc_pooled_hi95`, `auroc_pooled2_lo95` and `auroc_pooled2_hi95` so that they are not read as within-drug intervals. The JSON keys are unchanged.
   * `ddiood.config.SEEDS` was corrected from [1, 2, 3, 42] to [42, 1, 2, 3, 4], the seeds `ddiood.evaluate2` uses. Only the superseded `ddiood.evaluate` read it.
