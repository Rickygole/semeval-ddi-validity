## C1: model-free same-pair dissociation

### Question

On identical pairs, how far do three label sources agree: the SemEval 2013 sentence annotation, the FDA label rule that built the E7 pairs, and the DrugBank listing? Referee M3 argues that a score which fits one source and not another is evidence about the sources and not about drug knowledge. C1 gives the source-to-source agreement without any model.

### Inputs

- SemEval 2013 DDI corpus train and test, loaded with `ddiood.semeval.load_pairs(split)` (entity types `drug` and `drug_n`, `config.ENTITY_TYPES`). Fields used: `sentence_id`, `source` (folder name `DDI-DrugBank` or `DDI-MedLine`), `drug_a`, `drug_b`, `label_id` (positive when not 0).
- The SemEval vocabulary: the 992 keys of `artifacts/vocab/drug_lookup.json` (`lookup`), with `vocab_label_found` from `artifacts/labels/coverage.json`, as `scripts/amend7_build_pairs.py` passes it to `amend7_pairs.build_training_pairs`.
- FDA labels: `ddiood.openfda.LabelStore(offline=True)` over `data/fda_labels/`, the snapshot used for E7 (its dates are in `artifacts/amend7/train_pairs_report.json`, `audit.snapshot_training_labels`). The Drug Interactions section is `section_text(label, config.HOLDOUT_SECTION)`. The OOD sections in `artifacts/amend7/di_sections.json` (183 drugs) are not vocabulary drugs and are not used by C1.
- DrugBank 5.1 (`~/Downloads/drugbank_all_full_database.xml.zip`), read through the existing lookup code: `ddiood.drugbank.load_interactions`, `scripts/rev2_m3b.load_table` and `header` (version and export date written to every output) and `scripts/amend7_cpu_exp5_dissociation.resolve` with `forms`. Listed means `resolve` returns `positive`.

### Pair universe

A pair is an unordered pair of normalised names (each lowercased and stripped, the `pair_key` of `scripts/rev2_analyze_a.py`). A pair that occurs in several sentences is one pair. A pair in both train and test is one pair in the pooled analysis and appears once in each split row.

Entry conditions, counted step by step in the output:

1. both names are keys of `lookup` (otherwise dropped and counted);
2. at least one of the two vocabulary drugs has a non-empty Drug Interactions section in its FDA label;
3. the pair is not a self pair.

Self-pair convention, used in every table of C1: a self pair is a pair whose two names are equal after lowercasing and stripping (the convention of `results/revision/rev2/m3b_drugbank.md`, "no self pairs"). Self pairs are excluded from every primary and stratified table. The count of self pairs, and a full copy of the headline table with them included, are reported once as a separate table. Pairs with different names and the same RxCUI are kept, and their count is reported.

### Labels

SemEval pair labels, from all sentence instances of the pair in the rows of the table:

- `any`: the pair is positive if at least one instance has `label_id != 0`.
- `majority`: the pair is positive if strictly more than half of its instances have `label_id != 0`. A pair with exactly half positive instances is not positive under `majority`, and the number of such pairs is reported.

FDA-label pair label, the frozen E7 rule applied to a pair. The rule is the one that built `artifacts/amend7/train_pairs.csv`: `ddiood.ood.build_pairs(..., negatives="partner")` as called by `ddiood.amend7_pairs.build_training_pairs`. C1 reuses its building blocks without change (`ood.mention_index`, `ood.label_text`, `ood.label_identity`, `ood.NULL_EFFECT`, `ood._first_anchor`, `ood._window`, `textnorm.mentions`, `textnorm.guardrail_excluded`, `textnorm.has_risk_signal`, `openfda.section_text`, `vocab.surface_forms`) in a new function `rev3_pairlabel.pair_state(a, b)`. A direction is an ordered choice of anchor `x` and partner `y` among the two drugs. For a direction:

- the anchor gates of E7 apply: the anchor has a Drug Interactions section, is not rejected by `label_identity` (combination label or label for another moiety), and is not an anchor that E7 skips because its candidates are empty with a risk signal;
- the direction is E7-positive if `y` is an E7 candidate of `x` (a surface form of `y` found in the section by `mention_index`, `y` eligible: label found and name length at least 4, and neither name contained in the other) and a sentence mentioning `y` has an interaction anchor word in its window and is not a null-effect sentence;
- the direction is E7-negative if `x` is an E7 information anchor (it has at least one E7-positive partner, as E7 draws negatives only for anchors that have positives), `y` is not in the mentions of the whole label of `x`, the RxCUI of `y` is not among the RxCUIs mentioned in the label of `x`, `y` is not excluded by `guardrail_excluded` against the section of `x`, and `x` is not in the mentions of the whole label of `y` (the `negative_reverse_mention` filter).

The pair label is `positive` if either direction is E7-positive; `negative` if no direction is E7-positive and at least one is E7-negative; otherwise `undecided`. The per-anchor cap of ten positives and the 2:1 draw of negatives are sampling steps of E7, not labelling rules, and are not applied. Two consequences are reported, not hidden: pairs that E7 would never draw because of the cap are still labelled, and pairs whose only anchor has no positive partner are `undecided`.

Two sensitivity versions of the FDA label are reported beside the primary: (S1) the negative condition without the information-anchor requirement; (S2) the E7 exclusions of the 38 OOD-equivalent vocabulary drugs and of the OOD pair set (`amend7_pairs.excluded_vocab`, `ood_pair_set`) also applied, which reproduces the exact E7 universe.

Consistency gate, reported and not hidden: `pair_state` is run on all 4,643 pairs of `train_pairs.csv`. Every pair that E7 labelled positive must be `positive` and every pair that E7 labelled negative must be `negative` under S2. The counts of agreement and each disagreement type are written to the output. A disagreement does not stop the analysis; it is reported with the headline table.

DrugBank label: `listed` or `not listed`, with unresolvable pairs (neither name resolved) counted as `not listed` in the primary and shown as a third column. A sensitivity restricts to pairs for which both names resolve.

### Universe of the primary tables

Pairs that satisfy the entry conditions and have an FDA label of `positive` or `negative`. The pairs with FDA label `undecided` are not in the cross-tabulation; their number, their SemEval positive rate and their DrugBank listing rate are reported beside it so that the selection can be seen.

### Tables

Strata: split (train, test, pooled) and source folder (`DDI-DrugBank`, `DDI-MedLine`, both). A pair that occurs in both source folders is counted in both source rows and in the `both` row, never in an exclusive row, and the count of such pairs is reported.

1. Counts: the entry funnel, the pair counts by label, and the full three-way and four-way cross-tabulation (SemEval `any` by FDA by DrugBank, and the same with `majority`).
2. Agreement: for each of the six pairs of labels among {SemEval any, SemEval majority, FDA, DrugBank}, the raw agreement rate, Cohen's kappa, and the AUROC of each label used as a score for the other, in both directions. For a 0/1 score the AUROC of X for Y is 0.5 (1 + TPR - FPR) with Y as truth; the two directions are not equal and both are shown.
3. Instance view: the same agreement, kappa and AUROC with the SemEval label taken per sentence instance (instance positive when `label_id != 0`) against the FDA and DrugBank label of the instance's pair.
4. Self pairs included: the headline agreement table recomputed with self pairs, and the self-pair counts.
5. Sensitivities S1 and S2, and the both-resolved DrugBank restriction.

### Statistics

- Pair view: a pair-clustered percentile bootstrap, which resamples unique pairs with replacement, 1,000 draws, `numpy.random.RandomState(42)`, 95% percentile interval, for agreement, kappa and both AUROCs. Draws in which a statistic is undefined (a class missing) are dropped and counted.
- Instance view: a sentence-clustered bootstrap, which resamples `sentence_id` values with replacement, same draws and seed (`ddiood.evaluate2.boot_auroc` with sentence groups for the AUROCs; the same resampling for agreement and kappa).
- Strata with fewer than 20 pairs in a class report the estimate without an interval and are marked.
- No test is run and no label is declared the reference.

### Outputs

`results/revision/rev3/c1_dissociation.json` and `.md`. Per-pair table (names, three labels, source, instance count) in `runs/rev3/c1_pairs.csv`; the pickled DrugBank table cache in `runs/rev3/drugbank_table.pkl`. Neither is committed.

## C2: reference rows for the R2a seen cohort

### Cohorts

The seen cohort of `artifacts/amend7/train_pairs.csv`: all 4,643 pairs (1,542 positive; 299 anchor drugs, 313 partner drugs) and the 4,062 pairs that are not a pair in SemEval 2013 train. The second cohort is built by `scripts/rev2_analyze_a.overlap_summary` and `semeval_pair_index` unchanged (match rule: unordered pair of names, each lowercased and stripped, against every SemEval train instance; 581 pairs match, 4,062 do not). Both cohorts are reported and neither is preferred. The anchor is `drug_a` and the partner is `drug_b`, as in R2a. 175 drugs act as both anchor and partner, which is stated next to every interval.

### Rows

Each row is a score for the 4,643 or 4,062 pairs; the first four need no model and no R2a output.

1. `drugbank_lookup`: 1 if the pair is listed in DrugBank 5.1 by the experiment 5 rule (`resolve`), 0 otherwise (unresolvable counts as 0). The count of unresolvable and of one-side-resolved pairs is reported.
2. `label_string_match`: 1 if the partner is named anywhere outside the Drug Interactions section of the anchor's own FDA label (`baselines.non_di_text` and `baselines.Context.named`, the baseline of `results/corrected/baselines.json` applied to a vocabulary anchor). Stated in the output next to the row: E7 negatives are by construction pairs whose partner is not named anywhere in the anchor's label, so this score is 0 for every negative and the row is a property of the construction. The same construction note applies to the Novel and Older rows of `m3b_drugbank.md`.
3. `partner_prior_label_free`: the share of vocabulary Drug Interactions sections that name the partner (`baselines.Context.partner_prior`, which already excludes sections of the partner's own RxCUI), computed here with the anchor's own section also excluded, because the anchor's section defines the positives of the seen cohort. The version without that exclusion (the form used for the OOD cohorts) is reported as a second row and labelled as circular for these pairs.
4. `partner_rate_leave_one_drug_out`: the smoothed positive rate of the partner in the training labels, `(positives + 1) / (positives + negatives + 2)` as in `a7-prior-partner-train`, computed for each anchor drug `A` from all seen pairs in which `A` appears neither as anchor nor as partner. Because 175 drugs act in both roles, removing the held-out drug in both roles is what makes the score label-independent of that drug. On the 4,062-pair cohort, the rate is computed from the 4,062 pairs only, and a variant computed from all 4,643 pairs is reported beside it. The ordinary (not leave-out) partner rate is shown as a labelled circular reference.
5. The R2a rows, copied from `r2a.json` when it exists (b2mixed, b2sentence, marker; names only; seed mean), so that the reference rows and the model rows sit in one table. They are not recomputed by C2.

### Statistic and bootstrap

The within-drug AUROC by anchor drug, `amend7_analysis.fast_within` in mode `within`, with the two-way cluster bootstrap `amend7_analysis.draws` over anchor drugs and partner drugs, 1,000 draws, seed 42 (`BOOT["seen"]` and `BOOT["seen_not_in_semeval_train"]` of `scripts/rev2_analyze_a.py`), through `rev2_analyze_a.within` unchanged, 95% percentile interval, all rows sharing the same draws. Beside it: the pooled AUROC, and an anchor-only (one-way) bootstrap interval with the same seed, obtained by passing a constant partner vector to `draws`, because the two-way scheme treats a drug that is both anchor and partner as two independent clusters.

### Outputs

`results/revision/rev3/c2_seen_reference.json` and `.md`. No statement of equivalence or difference is made between the seen rows and the Novel or Older rows; the cohorts contain different drugs and are shown side by side only.

## C3: statistics

### (a) Holm family with the originally registered H1 endpoint

The preregistration says "Holm correction across H1 to H6" and Amendment 5, item 3, makes it concrete: only equivalence-tested hypotheses carry a p-value, H3 is descriptive, H2 and H5 are threshold-decided, so the family is H1, H4 and H6, with the Holm rule of `ddiood.evaluate2.holm` (sorted p, multiplier `m - rank`, running maximum, capped at 1, pending counts as 1).

C3(a) substitutes for H1 the endpoint as the original registration defines it: the pooled Novel OOD AUROC of the names-only `b2mixed` model, seed-averaged over seeds 1, 2, 3, 4 and 42, drug-cluster bootstrap, margin [0.40, 0.60]. Its p_TOST is read from `results/revision/rev2/m7_prereg_endpoint.json` (0.046; `ddiood.evaluate2.tost_p` on the 90% tails, Amendment 6 item 3). H4 and H6 keep their amended definitions and their stored p-values (`results/revision/eval/decisions.json`), because their original wording is not computable with the amended statistics. The script reports:

- the three raw p-values, the three Holm-adjusted p-values and the adjusted p of the substituted H1;
- whether H1 would be supported (adjusted p below 0.05 with the interval inside [0.40, 0.60]) and, separately, the reading of the original wording "primary endpoint interval inside [0.40, 0.60]" with the 95% interval ([0.457, 0.608]) and with the 90% interval;
- the same table for the amended family (H1 Older-clean, H4, H6) as filed, for comparison;
- the family size sensitivity: Holm with H4 or H6 removed from the family, to show how much of the adjustment comes from the other two tests.

Nothing is recomputed from predictions unless the stored p-value cannot be read; if it is recomputed (1,000 draws, seed 42, `ev.boot_auroc`), the script checks that it reproduces 0.529 [0.457, 0.608] and p 0.046.

### (b) Panel between-model to sampling variance ratio with the one-way drug bootstrap

Inputs and structure as in `scripts/post8_e9.py` and `ddiood.amend7_panel.panel_stats`: the 20 Amendment 7 panel checkpoints (marker and pair formats, `variant` not masked or external), names-only inputs `ood_empty` (Novel, 438 rows, 48 drugs) and `oodold_empty` (Older-primary), tiers A and C-auto, within-drug AUROC per checkpoint.

The two-way draws of that code weight a tetrad `(drug, positive partner, negative partner)` by `w1[drug] * w2[qpos] * w2[qneg]`. The one-way version keeps `w1` (OOD drugs resampled with replacement, copies as separate strata as in Amendment 6 item 6) and sets `w2` to ones, so partners are held fixed. 1,000 draws, seeds 1042 (Novel) and 42 (Older-primary), draws shared by all checkpoints.

Reported per cohort and tier, for both bootstrap schemes side by side: the between-checkpoint variance of the point AUROCs, the mean single-checkpoint bootstrap variance, `ratio_old = between / mean single variance`, `paired_within_reference = mean over checkpoint pairs of Var(A_i - A_j) / 2`, `ratio_paired = between / paired_within_reference`, the mean bootstrap covariance, and the number of checkpoints with 95% upper bound at most 0.60. Check: the two-way values must reproduce `results/revision/amend7/e9.json` and `results/revision/post8/e9.json`; a mismatch is reported.

The one-way scheme is the near-nominal one in the coverage simulations (`m8_coverage.md`: ratio 0.933 to 1.207 for the one-way drug bootstrap), and the two-way scheme is conservative (1.5 to 2.3 times nominal). The ratio is therefore expected to differ; no direction is assumed.

### (c) Spearman rho of SemEval F1 against OOD agreement

Quantities per checkpoint: the SemEval binary F1 (`ddiood.metrics.binary_f1` of `test_sentence` predictions against `label_id != 0`) and the OOD agreement, taken here to be the within-drug AUROC of the names-only score against the tier A label (the agreement of the score with the FDA label positives, as in M4(a)), for Novel and Older-primary. The agreement with DrugBank-normalised kappa of E9 is a monotone rescaling by the shared DrugBank AUROC and gives the same point rho; it is reported as a check. The tier C-auto version is reported beside it. This reading of "OOD agreement" is an interpretation of the referee text and is flagged for the authors.

For all 20 checkpoints, and for the marker and pair strata (10 each), three intervals for the same rho:

1. the shared-draw interval of `panel_stats`: Spearman rho recomputed on each shared bootstrap draw of the 20 AUROCs, 1,000 draws, percentile 95% (this includes sampling noise of the OOD set and ignores checkpoint-to-checkpoint variability); both bootstrap schemes (two-way as submitted, one-way from (b));
2. the checkpoint bootstrap of `scripts/rev2_m4.py`: resample checkpoints within the stratum, 10,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`, percentile 95%, degenerate draws dropped and counted; with the permutation p of 10,000 permutations, `RandomState(43)`. Must reproduce `m4_partner.md`;
3. a nested interval, shown as a third column and labelled as an addition: each of 10,000 draws resamples checkpoints and one shared bootstrap draw, then computes rho. It combines both sources of uncertainty.

### (d) Power statement for the panel, by simulation

Question: given the observed sampling variance of the 20 within-drug AUROCs, what correlation between SemEval F1 and OOD AUROC could the panel have detected with 80% power?

Model for one simulated panel, per cohort and tier: `A_i = mu + beta * z_i + e_i + u_i`, where `z_i` is the observed SemEval F1 of checkpoint `i`, standardised across the panel (kept fixed), `e` is multivariate normal with mean 0 and the empirical covariance of the shared bootstrap draws (so the observed sampling variances and the correlation across checkpoints are used), `u_i` is normal with variance `tau^2` for extra between-model variation not explained by F1, and `mu` the observed mean AUROC. Two settings: `tau = 0` (the most favourable to detection) and `tau` equal to the observed between-checkpoint standard deviation. `beta` runs over a grid from 0 to 0.08 in steps of 0.005 AUROC per standard deviation of F1. For each `beta`, 5,000 simulated panels, `RandomState(2026)`; in each, the Spearman rho between `z` and the simulated `A` and its two-sided asymptotic p-value (`scipy.stats.spearmanr`) at alpha 0.05.

Reported: the power curve; the smallest `beta` with power at least 0.80 and the expected observed Spearman rho at that `beta` (the "detectable rho"); the same for 10 checkpoints per format; and the power at the observed rho values. Negative and positive signs are symmetric by construction. The simulation says what the panel could detect and says nothing about what the true correlation is.

### Outputs

`results/revision/rev3/c3_statistics.json` and `.md`, with the four parts as sections.

### Items from TAC, by rules that do not use the model

Calibration set (primary): the 31 labels of test set 1 and the 40 labels of test set 2 that have a Drug Interactions section (sentences with `section="34073-7"`). Training-22 (16 labels with such a section) is reported separately as a sensitivity and never pooled, because its annotation is semi-automatic and incomplete.

- The prompt section is the text of the `Section` element with `id="34073-7"` of the label; `{drug}` is the `drug` attribute of the `Label` element, verbatim.
- Gold positive: a distinct (label, partner) such that the partner is the normalised `str` of a `Precipitant` mention in a Drug Interactions sentence that is the precipitant of at least one `Interaction` of that sentence. Partner normalisation: lowercase, whitespace collapsed. All three interaction types count as interaction. The partner shown in the prompt is the normalised string.
- Partner type: `substance` if the mention code is a UNII (ten alphanumeric characters, case-insensitive), `class` otherwise (NDF-RT NUI "N" followed by ten digits, NONE, NO MAP, NOT_DRUG). Primary analysis: `substance`, because R2b partners are named drugs. Secondary: `class`, and all partners together.
- Gold negative, partner-matched as in E7 (`ood.partner_matched_negatives`): for each gold positive (label `L`, partner `X`) in the same test set, the candidate labels `d` are the other labels in the calibration set with a Drug Interactions section such that the normalised `X` does not occur as a substring of the normalised text of any section provided in `d`'s file, no `Mention` of `d` has the code of `X` (when `X` has a code other than NONE, NO MAP and NOT_DRUG), and `d` is not named `X`. Up to `NEGATIVE_RATIO = 2` candidates are drawn for each positive with `random.Random(f"17:{L}:{X}")`, without drawing the same (label, partner) twice. The TAC file holds only some sections of each label (all of them in test set 1, the Drug Interactions and Clinical Pharmacology sections in test set 2), so "never named" is checked against the provided text, which is the text the adjudicator sees plus other provided sections.
- Items are unique (label, partner); the item list is serialised with sorted keys and its sha256 recorded before the model loads; a rerun asserts it.
- The TAC annotation has no explicit negative annotation, so gold negatives mean "the annotated section of that label and the provided text do not name the partner". This is the same meaning that R2b's negatives have, and it is a limit: a label can omit an interaction that is real. It is stated with every specificity.
- Expected size (counted while planning, not final): about 1,940 distinct gold positives over the two test sets (about 620 substance partners and about 1,300 class partners) and up to twice as many negatives, so about 5,800 prompts.

### Statistics

- Decision: `yes` against not `yes`. Primary treatment of `unsure` and of non-answers: counted as not `yes`. Two sensitivities: `unsure` and non-answers excluded; `unsure` counted as `yes`.
- Sensitivity Se = P(decision `yes` | gold positive) and specificity Sp = P(decision not `yes` | gold negative), with Clopper-Pearson exact 95% intervals (`scripts/rev2_score_adjudicator.py` helper), for: primary (substance partners, test sets 1 and 2 pooled); class partners; all partners; each test set alone; Training-22; and by partner type crossed with section length tertile (descriptive). Because items of one label share a section, a label-cluster bootstrap interval (resample labels, 2,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`) is reported beside every Clopper-Pearson interval, which assumes independent items.
- The counts of `unsure`, of non-answers, of chunked items and the Youden index `Se + Sp - 1` are reported.
- Rogan-Gladen correction of the R2b random sample (the 250 `S` items of `{D}/runs/preds/r2-olmo-adjudication.json`, by cohort and by label class; the audit sheet items are not corrected, because they were selected for suspected errors). For each cohort and label class the observed share of `yes` decisions `q` is the sample rate. For label class 1 the estimated true share of gold positives is `t1 = (q + Sp - 1) / (Se + Sp - 1)` and the corrected error rate of the positive labels is `1 - t1`; for label class 0 the corrected error rate of the negative labels is `t0 = (q + Sp - 1) / (Se + Sp - 1)`. Estimates are clipped to [0, 1], the unclipped value is also reported, and a note is written when the Youden index is below 0.3 (the correction is then unstable). Interval: a Monte Carlo of 10,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`, drawing `q` from a binomial with the observed rate and sample size, and `Se` and `Sp` from the label-cluster bootstrap of the TAC calibration, 95% percentile of the corrected rate. Uncorrected rates with Clopper-Pearson intervals are shown beside the corrected ones.
- Assumptions stated in the output: Se and Sp measured on TAC label sections transfer to the OpenFDA sections of the OOD drugs (the two differ in length, drug class and section format); the TAC gold is itself an annotation and not an oracle; the model is the frozen R2b model, and its pretraining data were not audited for these label texts.

### D1: R2c transport

Question: do the corrected rates of C4 depend on which calibration is used, and is the model's observed yes rate on the R2b positives compatible with the sensitivity measured on TAC?

Inputs: `results/revision/rev3/c4_calibration.json` (calibration counts, Se, Sp, intervals, tercile edges `length_edges`, `tac_items_sha256`); `runs/rev3/` per-item TAC decisions as used by `scripts/rev3_score_tac.py` (the items file named there, read with its `load` function) for the label-cluster bootstrap draws; the 250 random-sample items (`set` equal to the value `rev3_score_tac.rogan_gladen` selects as `random`) of `runs/preds/r2-olmo-adjudication.json` with fields `cohort`, `current_label`, `decision`, `non_answer`, `tokens`. (a) Test-set-specific Rogan-Gladen. For `test1_substance` and for `test2_substance` of `c4_calibration.json` (substance partners, the stratum matching the named drugs of R2b), apply `rev3_score_tac.rogan_gladen` unchanged to the random-sample items, giving the corrected rates by cohort (Older, Novel) and label class (1 and 0), plus the unweighted pool by class as in C4. (b) Length-matched correction. Edges are `length_edges` (882, 1485). A random-sample item with `tokens` at most 882 is in tercile 1, at most 1485 in tercile 2, otherwise tercile 3, using the same less-or-equal rule as `rev3_score_tac.length_terciles`. The TAC terciles were assigned per label from the longest item of the label; the R2b items are assigned from their own prompt length. This difference is stated in the output. The tercile calibrations are the strata `substance_length_tercile_1`, `_2` and `_3` of C4, with the sensitivity and specificity of each. The point estimate is reported unclipped and clipped. The per-tercile counts of the sample by cohort and class are reported; a tercile with fewer than 20 calibration items in a class is marked.

### D6: sentence-dependence tests on the R2a marker models

Question: does the 0.984 pooled AUROC of the marker models depend on the sentence and on the drug names, as measured by the same tests used on the weaker models?

Inputs. SemEval 2013 test pairs through `gpu_jobs.Inputs` (2,810 pairs with entity types `config.ENTITY_TYPES`, gold `label_id`, source and `sentence_id`), the offsets attached as in `rev2_encoders.encoder_job` (`pn.attach_offsets(inp.test, pn.load_offsets("test"))`), and the five saved marker models for seeds `rev2_encoders.SEEDS` (42, 1, 2, 3, 4), loaded from `{D}/weights/r2-marker-biomedbert-s{seed}` after the sha256 of the weight file equals the one stored in `runs/preds/r2-marker-biomedbert-s{seed}.json`. Input format is the marker format `<e1>a</e1> and <e2>b</e2>.` of `amend7_panel.marker_names` and the marked sentence of `amend7_panel.marker_sentence`. Inputs per seed, six in all:

1. `sentence`: the original marked sentence, `marker_sentence(inp.test)`.
2. `masked`: `inputs.mask_names(x, [drug_a, drug_b])` applied to each marked sentence, which is the masking of the existing masked evaluation (`inputs.b2_masked`: every occurrence of either name, case-insensitive, word boundaries as in `mask_names`, replaced by `@DRUG$`) and therefore replaces the two names inside the markers as well as other occurrences, leaving the tags.
3. `swap_vocab`, 4. `swap_novel`, 5. `swap_pseudo`: `marker_sentence` of the rows `inp.swaps["vocab"]`, `["novel"]`, `["pseudo"]` built by `gpu_jobs.Inputs._swaps` (frozen, `random.Random(0)`, names drawn from the SemEval vocabulary, the Novel OOD drug names, or pseudo words from `inputs.pseudo_word`) through `inputs.swapped`. The swapped rows keep the gold label. Stored annotated offsets no longer match after a swap, so `mark` falls back to the name search of `amend7_panel.mark`; rows for which no valid marking exists use the template-plus-sentence fallback of `marker_sentence`, and the number of such rows is stored per input and seed.
6. `names`: `marker_names(inp.test)`, the names-only marker input.

Reproduction checks inside the job, before scoring: the `sentence` probabilities reproduce the stored `test_sentence` probabilities of the same seed to a maximum absolute gap of 1e-3, and `names` reproduces the stored `test_empty`; a larger gap fails the job.

Statistic. Per seed and input: pooled AUROC of the positive-class probability `1 - P(no interaction)` (the `p` of `bert.predict`) against `label_id != 0` (`evaluate2.auroc`), and binary F1 of `preds != 0` against `label_id != 0`. Reported: per seed values, the seed mean of each, and the AUROC of the seed-mean probability. Differences from the `sentence` input (masked minus sentence, and so on) are reported with the same draws. For the swap inputs also the flip rate of the binary prediction against the `sentence` input of the same seed and the mean absolute change in probability (as `evaluate2.swap_rows`). Intervals: sentence-cluster bootstrap over `sentence_id`, 1,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`, one resample applied to all seeds and inputs, percentile 95%, on the seed-mean of the per-seed metric. Scoring is deterministic (evaluation mode), so no scoring seed exists; the seeds are the five training seeds.

Five-class micro-F1: on the `sentence` input, the argmax predictions `preds` of each seed through `evaluate`, `micro` and `boot` of `scripts/rev2_m10.py` unchanged (micro F1 over mechanism, effect, advise and int, the 2,810-pair set, the official-set bound with the same `extra_fn`, sentence-cluster bootstrap 1,000 draws, seed 42), computed from the new prediction files and, as a check, from the stored `runs/preds` ones, which must agree to 6 decimals; the output states the agreement.

Job. `ddiood/rev3b_jobs.py` follows `rev2_jobs`: registry of five jobs named `r3b-marker-sentence-s{seed}`, group `R3b`, `CODE_FILES`, `code_version`, `config_hash` with the `smoke` flag, `env_info`, `preflight` (free disk, GPU present, weights directory), atomic writes through `amend7_jobs.atomic_write` to `{D}/runs/preds/{name}.json`, skip of finished jobs by `finished`, a `.failed.txt` file on error, and a stored block with the weight sha256 check, the six inputs' `preds`, `p` and the marking-fallback counts. The notebook generator produces `colab/experiments/R3b_marker_sentence.ipynb` (code cells only, Drive always mounted, an assertion that a GPU is present, `flush_and_unmount` as the last cell, frozen-hash checks of the code files and the bundle). Time estimate stated before running: about 17,000 inferences per seed on a base-size encoder, minutes per seed on an A100. A CPU smoke test uses a tiny stand-in encoder and a subsample. The scorer `scripts/rev3_d6.py` reads only downloaded prediction files from `runs/preds` and writes `results/revision/rev3/d6_marker_sentence.json` and `.md`; if a seed is missing the row is marked incomplete.

### D7: human-gold check of the label rule

Question: where our label-mention rule and the TAC 2018 human annotation cover the same drug, do they agree?

Inputs. TAC test1 and test2 parsed by `ddiood.rev3_tac.load_set` for labels with a Drug Interactions section (gold: distinct (label, partner) with the partner a normalised `Precipitant` string of an `Interaction` in a Drug Interactions sentence; partner type from the code as in `partner_type`; primary stratum `substance`). Our side: the pair files `artifacts/ood/ood_pairs.csv` (Novel) and `artifacts/ood/ood_old_pairs.csv` (Older), cohorts Novel, Older-clean and Older-primary as defined by `amend7_analysis.Truth.cohort`, with label 1 (label-mention positive) and the partner-matched negatives (`no_interaction_silver`).

Overlap rule. A TAC label drug and an OOD drug overlap if their names are equal after `rev3_tac.norm` (lowercase, whitespace collapsed). No salt stripping and no first-word matching are used for inference. Minimum: at least 5 overlapping drugs in at least one cohort. Below the minimum the output records `check_possible: false`, the overlap counts per cohort and per TAC set, the first-word overlap count as a count only, and nothing else; no agreement figure is computed. The count stated above is 0 (Novel), 4 (Older-clean) and 4 (Older-primary) under this rule, so the expected outcome is that the check is not possible, and the plan commits to that outcome rather than to the looser rule, which would give exactly 5 and is mentioned only as a count.

If the minimum is met (not expected): for the overlapping drugs, the pair universe is every (drug, partner) of our pair file for those drugs. Partner match: our `semeval_drug` string against the normalised TAC partner string, equality after `norm`; TAC gold partners are restricted to substance partners whose normalised string is a name in the union of our partner vocabularies. Reported: agreement (share of our pairs on which our label equals TAC gold positive membership), precision of our positives (share of our positives that TAC gold lists), recall (share of TAC gold substance positives in the restricted universe that our positives contain), the share of our partner-matched negatives that TAC gold lists, each with a Clopper-Pearson 95% interval and, if at least 10 overlapping labels, a label-cluster bootstrap (2,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`, resampling drugs). The comparison carries the limits that the TAC label version may differ from the label version we parsed, that TAC partner strings may name a substance by a synonym we do not match (so precision is a lower bound), and that a pair absent from TAC gold is not an annotated negative. Outputs: `results/revision/rev3/d7_tac_overlap.json` and `.md`, with no drug name, partner or label text; only counts and rates.

### D8: consequence of the partner anti-correlation for the evaluated systems

Cohorts: Older-clean (`Truth.cohort("older_clean")`, 1,832 pairs, 128 drugs), Novel (`Truth.cohort("novel")`) and the seen cohort (4,643 pairs of `artifacts/amend7/train_pairs.csv`, via `rev2_analyze_a.cohorts`). Systems and scores, each on a cohort only where the score exists: the stored b2mixed and b2sentence families (`results/revision/preds`, key `ood_empty` or `oodold_empty`, Novel and Older only), the R2a families b2mixed, b2sentence and marker (`runs/preds`, keys `ood_empty`, `oodold_empty`, `train_empty`, seed-mean probability through `rev2_analyze_a.seed_p`, the seeds present listed), the Qwen names-only run (`runs/preds/a7-qwen-names.json`, keys `ood` and `oodold` of the primary prompt, Novel and Older only) and the DrugBank lookup as scored in C2 and `post7_partner` (0 or 1, unresolvable counted as not listed). No new model run.

Exclusion rule: for a pair (anchor A, partner y) the other pairs of y are those of `rev3_c2.lodo_scores`: pairs of y with anchor A removed and, in the seen cohort, pairs of y whose partner position is held by A as an anchor-partner as in that function. Two scores per pair from the other pairs of y: the leave-one-drug-out partner positive rate (`rev3_c2` smoothed rate `(pos + 1) / (n + 2)`, so that D8 links to C2's 0.061) and the system's partner-marginal score, the unweighted mean of the system's score over the same other pairs. Pairs with no other pair of the partner have the marginal undefined; they are dropped from the statistics and counted.

Statistics. (a) Spearman correlation across pairs between the system's partner-marginal score and the leave-one-drug-out positive rate, with a partner-cluster percentile bootstrap (resample partner drugs, 1,000 draws, `RandomState(42)`, 95%), and the same correlation at partner level, one row per partner with at least 2 pairs, between the partner's mean score over all its pairs and its positive rate over all its pairs (the latter two use no leave-one-out and are labelled as such). (b) Within-drug AUROC (`amend7_analysis.point(y, s, g, "within")`) of the system's partner-marginal score as a scorer of the pair label, with the two-way cluster bootstrap of `aa.draws` (1,000 draws, seed `aa.BOOT_SEED[cohort]` for Older-clean and Novel and 42 for seen, 95% percentile, scores held fixed across draws as in C2), beside the system's own within-drug AUROC and C2's rate scorer. The interpretation printed is limited to the descriptive question whether a partner tendency that needs no labels lies below 0.5, and states that the partner-marginal score of a system uses other anchors' pairs and so is a scorer only in the sense that the partner-matched label construction would reward it. Outputs: `results/revision/rev3/d8_partner_marginal.json` and `.md`.

### D9: DrugBank listing rates of SemEval test pairs under one convention

Inputs and resolver: as `scripts/rev2_m3b.py` (`load_table` with the cache `runs/rev3/drugbank_table.pkl`, DrugBank 5.1 export of 2026-03-05, header via `m3b.header`), `resolve` of `amend7_cpu_exp5_dissociation`, the vocabulary lookup `artifacts/vocab/drug_lookup.json`, SemEval 2013 test pairs (2,810 rows with `source` and `label_id`), `rates` and the Wilson interval `rc.wilson` as m3b. Self pairs: names equal after `drugbank._norm` (lowercase and strip), as m3b. Unordered pairs: `resolve` is order independent; the primary rows count SemEval test instances (rows), which reproduces the m3b denominators, and a reference block de-duplicates to distinct unordered pairs by `rev2_analyze_a.pair_key`, assigning a pair to each class in which it has an instance (the number of pairs with both classes is reported).

Rows: negatives and positives, each pooled and by source (DDI-DrugBank, DDI-MedLine), self pairs excluded (primary convention), and the same rows with self pairs included (reference). Per row: n, resolvable, listed, listed share of resolvable with Wilson 95% (the primary number), listed share of all n with Wilson 95% (the denominator of the AUROC table), and the number of self pairs removed. A table maps each existing value (0.763, 0.727, 0.785) to its row and denominator so the text can use one denominator. The Wilson intervals treat instances as independent (sentence clustering is not applied; the output says so). Outputs: `results/revision/rev3/d9_listing_conventions.json` and `.md`.
